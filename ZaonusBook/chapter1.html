Chapter One

The Tavern of the Silver Hearth - Oakenwood

The tavern quieted slowly, like a fire settling to coals. Voices lowered. Dice paused in uncertain hands. Even the wind against the shutters seemed to hush as Elara let the final note of her song drift into the rafters. She did not rush the ending. She never did. The sound thinned and faded, leaving only the crackle of the hearth and the faint scrape of boots on wood.

For a breath, there was silence. Then the room answered her.

Applause rose from the common room. Tankards struck tables in approval. A traveler near the hearth laughed and wiped at his eyes as though the smoke had troubled him.

Elara inclined her head in a modest bow. “Thank you.”

“Another!” someone called.

“Tomorrow,” she replied, smiling.

She set her viol upon its stand and stepped down from the small platform near the fire. Song gave way to work. She crossed behind the bar, tied her apron tighter at her waist, and reached for the waiting tray.

“Three ciders,” came the call from the kitchen door. “And two dark ales.”

“Coming.”

She poured with steady hands. The tavern of Oakenwood was full that evening. Traders bound for the western road, farmers disputing seed prices in low voices, and a scattering of strangers carrying the dust of travel on their cloaks. It was a good crowd. Honest noise. Familiar faces. And generous, after a song. She carried the drinks out among them.

“You perform as if you belong in a lord’s hall,” one merchant said as she set down his cider. “What are you doing here?”

Elara gave him a patient look. “Pouring your drink, it seems.”

He laughed. “You know what I mean.”

She did. She had sung in halls of carved stone and blazing chandeliers. She had stood before nobility and felt their approval settle like coin at her feet.

“Life is simple here,” she said. “I like it.”

The merchant shook his head, unconvinced. Some people were always reaching for the next rung, even when their footing was already sure.

At the corner table, Ren leaned forward with bright attention. The dark fur along her arms softened the lantern light around her. A book lay open beside her untouched stew, ink staining her fingertips.

“You altered the third verse,” Ren said as Elara passed. “Just slightly.”

“Did I?”

“You lifted it out of the minor before it settled too deeply.”

Trikx took a slow drink from his cup. “She knows when sorrow has lingered long enough.”

Ren considered that, then nodded.

“Drink your stew before it grows cold,” Elara said.

Trikx’s gaze drifted toward the door as it opened. It was a slight movement, nearly invisible to anyone not watching for it.

A rush of cool air entered first, carrying the scent of road dust. Two travelers stepped inside. No one paid them much attention. Oakenwood stood at the meeting of trade paths, once the main road between Emberhold and the elven nation of Enslon before the war narrowed its traffic. Strangers were common here.

Only one thing marked the pair as unusual. They were too short for Humans and not broad enough for Dwarves. Halflings were rare in Ethal. But Elara’s presence here had tempered the reaction to them when they happened by.

Elara reached for two clean tankards without looking up. “Find a table wherever you like.”

They paused just within the threshold before choosing a table near the center of the room. Their cloaks were travel-worn but finely made. The man carried himself with quiet authority. The woman’s bearing was careful, composed, despite visible fatigue.

Elara approached with her usual ease. “What may I bring you?”

The man removed his gloves. “Two ales.” His voice was steady.

She turned to pour. The cadence lingered in her thoughts. It was measured, deliberate. It stirred something she had not felt for years. She returned and set the tankards before them. The woman had not taken her eyes from her.

“Ara.”

The name was spoken softly.

Her hand tightened around the rim of the tankard. Ale slipped over the edge and dampened her fingers. No one had called her that in a decade.

She looked up. Time had altered them. Silver threaded the man’s hair. Care had sharpened the woman’s features. Yet some things endured. The thin scar along the base of his thumb. The familiar crease between her mother’s brows when uncertainty took hold.

The tavern continued around them. Laughter rose near the hearth. Someone called for more bread. A chair scraped against the floor. But, Elara heard none of it.

The last time she had seen their faces had been beneath banners and judgment. She had watched a verdict delivered. Watched the world she knew collapse inward. Ten years had passed since that day. It felt, in that moment, like yesterday.

“You have grown,” her mother said. The words settled heavily between them.

Elara placed the second tankard carefully upon the table. She wiped her hands on her apron and straightened. She had built something here. A life measured and honest. A name unshadowed.

“Welcome to Oakenwood,” she said. The courtesy was precise. She turned and stepped away without waiting for a reply, tending another table as though nothing had shifted.

Ren had risen halfway from her seat. “Elara?”

Elara did not look at her. “I am working,” she said quietly.

Trikx stood more slowly. He said nothing, but his attention sharpened. His gaze moved from Elara to the newcomers and back again, clearly trying to size up the situation and gauge the threat.

After serving a few other tables and replenishing her friends' drinks, Elara gathered herself and returned to the center table.

“We did not come for lodging, Ara,” the man said.

“I assumed not.”

Her mother leaned forward, then hesitated. “We hoped to speak with you.”

“You should order food first,” Elara replied. “The kitchen is still open.” Her tone was even. Professional.

The woman searched her face for something softer and did not find it. “Ara,” she began again.

“I am working,” Elara said. “If you do not intend to eat, others are waiting.” A faint edge touched her voice before she mastered it. They exchanged a glance and fell silent. Elara inclined her head and turned away.

Ren reached for her arm as she passed, but Elara shifted just enough that the touch did not land. “Who are they?” Ren asked.

“Travelers. Not here.”

A moment later, Ren’s voice brushed her thoughts, gentle and controlled.
Elara, is everything well?

The contact was faint, as it always was. A thread that would not hold long.
Yes, Ren. We’ll talk later. Privately.

She did not know how much of the truth traveled along that thread, but Ren had always been perceptive.

At the corner table, Trikx watched her return to the bar. He saw the rigid set of her shoulders and the tension in her hands as they closed around the polished wood. He glanced toward Ren. She gave a slight shake of her head. Not yet. He took the cue and settled back down like an unseen sentry, watching these newcomers for signs of danger.

At the center of the room, the two travelers sat before untouched ale—ghosts, warmed by hearthlight.

The tavern carried on in its ordinary rhythm, unaware that something long buried had stepped quietly back into the light.
