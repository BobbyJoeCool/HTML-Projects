Chapter Two

Elara’s Home - Oakenwood

“I don’t know why they are here. I don’t know what they want. Maybe you can use that magic of yours and find out?”

Ren’s expression hardened immediately. “You know I cannot. You know full well it’s not only unlawful, but wrong to enter another's mind without their consent.”

Elara looked to the floor. Of course, Ren would answer that way. And of course, she knew that. She didn’t want to stoop to her parents' level to get what she wanted. “Last I saw them, they were being led to prison. I thought I’d never have to see them or deal with them again!” The anger in Elara’s voice surprised Ren. Elara is rarely not in full command of her emotions.

Trikx seemed less surprised by this outburst. He chimed in quietly from the corner, “You know, prisons are not impregnable. It would be simple to check for an escape. Or notify the authorities.” He sneered at the word. “And there are other ways to make someone… disappear.”

Ren’s tail lashed wildly, and her ears folded back sharply as she scowled at Trikx. “They’re her family, you unfeeling goblin!” The word hung in the air and her tail fell flat to the floor. Trikx gave a slight grin to Ren, revealing a few too many of his sharp teeth.

Elara wasn’t sure which was more unsettling to her: Trikx's suggestion, Ren’s answer, or that a part of her desired it.

“And? She’s our friend, and they’re clearly causing her pain. We can stop that.”

Elara sensed that this was about to get out of hand. “Guys, enough.” She took a deep breath before continuing. “I know they’re my parents, but they raised me to be a pawn of their petty theft. Their irresponsibility left me alone in the world and forced me to make my own way. And dammit, I think I did pretty well.”

Ren turned to Elara and spoke softly, “They’re blood. Does that not mean anything to you?”

The blood left Elara’s face. “I was three when they first had me sing to distract a tourist while they lifted a purse. I was seven when they first booked me to perform at a tavern while they lifted the till. I was nine when they had me perform for hours on end in a town square while they took purses from dozens of passersby.” A single tear was streaming down her cheek. “I was sixteen when their thieving ways finally destroyed my entire life. Blood didn’t save me then. I saved myself. And I will not be dragged back into the mud by blood.”

Ren opened her mouth as if to speak, but closed it silently. Trikx from his darker spot in the corner looked away for a change, taking in the whole story. It was Trikx who broke the silence.

“Sounds to me like they taught you self-reliance.” He refocused his gaze on her. “How to survive in a world that doesn’t give a damn about people like us.”

Elara’s jaw tightened, and she shot a glance at Trikx, and Ren shifted in her seat uncomfortably.

“Trikx!” Ren interjected. She was no longer surprised by Trikx's brutal honesty, but she was still jarred by it. She turned her attention to Elara. “I was going to say they taught you how to stand on your own feet.”

“That’s what I said, isn’t it?” replied Trikx.

Ren rose to her feet; her fur stood on end. “No!” Ren looked to Elara, who was staring half blankly, taking in both sides of what her friends were telling her. “Your parents misused your talents. You did not! Strength born of hardship is still strength. Your strength is your own, Elara. You don’t owe them anything.”

Trikx studied Ren. A slight smile came to his face. “It doesn’t change the fact that they are the ones who taught it to her. Say what you want about thieves, we’re survivors.”

Ren glared at Trikx. “Why do you have to use logic against me? The end doesn’t justify the means! Abuse is still abuse, even if it set her down the path she is on now.”

Elara looked at Ren, a little bit shocked that those words came out of her mouth. She had never really thought about it that way before. Abuse. But this conversation had strayed too far from the issue that was important at this very moment. “Guys. Stop.” She spoke softly, but it got both of their attention.

“None of this is helping solve the problem in front of us right now,” she thought for a second and said again, softer this time, “in front of me.” She paused. Ren sat back down and lowered her head. “My parents are here. Now. And I have a choice in front of me, and I don’t know what to do.”

Ren and Trikx both simply stared at Elara. The decision sat heavily in her chest.

Ren chimed in first, “Whatever you want to do, we are here for you.” Ren looked to Trikx.

“Of course I’m here for you, Silver. But you know as well as I do. If they’re here, it’s because they need something. Tread carefully.”

Ren scowled at Trikx. Elara sometimes wished she could see things in the positive, hopeful way the Ren could. But Trikx was right. If Reeda and Cobbin were here, they would want something. She wondered if she even cared what they wanted. She did know she wanted to close this chapter of her life again. For good this time.

Just then, a rapid knock at the door followed by a soft voice that rang in her head, “Elara.” The warmth brushed her like the chorus of a familiar song, but an inevitable key change was coming. She drew a deep breath, swallowed hard, and her voice unexpectedly grew thin: “Come in, Seraphina.”
