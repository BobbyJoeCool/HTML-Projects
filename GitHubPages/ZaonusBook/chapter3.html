Chapter Three

The Letter

The letter lay open on the table between the four of them. The words were running through Elara’s mind. “We heard of your success…We’re proud of you…Jory is scheduled to be executed for treason.” Trikx was right on the nose; they want something, alright.

“They seem genuine in their desire to reconnect.” Elara scoffed at Seraphina. She looked right into the eyes of the blue raven on her shoulder.

“That’s only because you don’t know them. They’re con artists. They lie for a living. I already know their plan. They want me to be a distraction while they break Jory out.” The raven cocked its head sharply, and the look on Seraphina’s face darkened.

Trikx was sitting silently in the corner, twirling his dagger idly. Ren was reading the letter for the hundredth time. “It’s not going to change, kitten,” Trikx muttered. One ear twitched at the word, but she kept her focus on the letter.

“I’m studying it for hidden meanings or…or something we missed.”

“We didn’t miss anything, Ren. They’re here. They want my help with my brother Jory. But they also are not telling me everything.” Her thoughts went back to the words she had only ever heard them say when they had a good day of lifting purses. We’re proud of you. “They’re also not proud of me. They see an opportunity to use me again. And what did Jory do while in prison to get charged with treason?”

Trikx paused his fidgeting for a second. “Turn the tables. Manipulate them. Use that bardic magic you learned from Mr. Nice Guy and get the truth from them.”

Ren shifted very uncomfortably at this suggestion. The raven immediately shot a piercing stare in Trikx's direction. Elara felt something undeniable in that moment: temptation. They’d pulled her strings for sixteen years. Scheduled every performance to line up with their ambitions. Smoothed her hair, shaped her smile, positioned her like a pawn in a game of Dragonchess. Why shouldn’t she call the moves for once?

This thought unfurled inside her, filling her with a warmth unlike anything she had felt in a long time. She could see it. She had done it before when she served Lord [Noble] in the tribunals. Weaving the words of the Goddess Gratia with her own, she used to soften the resistance of the accused and draw confessions. That was justice. A service to the crown. Bringing truth to the light for the good of those victimized.

This was not that; this was personal. In the tribunals, it was about shining light on the truth. But this, a desire to force her parents to lay everything to bear. She could make them squirm. She could make them tell her how they really feel. Do they really feel remorse for using her like a pawn, or did they just see her as a way to make money? She could draw the guilt to the surface and use her magic to make it so intense that they would practically drown in their own tears. The thought intoxicated her with bitter warmth, and she liked how it felt.

“Elara…” It was the gentle call of Ren’s voice that called her back to reality. The warmth turned to guilt in an instant. She scolded herself for thinking that way. Did Ren know what I was thinking, or was it just fortune that she snapped me out of that?

Trikx spoke up again, “You know, Ren, you don’t always have to do the right thing. Idealism comes with no reward. You could choose to let go and ignore this completely. No one would think less of you.”

Seraphina’s raven’s eyes, still fixed on Trikx, narrowed, and she spoke up, “Trikx, sometimes you have to do the thing that isn’t the pragmatic choice.” Trikx scowled at her. Unlike Ren and Elara, Trikx looked at her, instead of the raven she used to see. “And none of this helps solve the dilemma at hand. Jory is in trouble, and Elara needs to decide if she wants to help or to let go.”

Elara looked up at the ceiling and rubbed her temples. “And I’m avoiding that decision, aren’t I?” Ren nodded silently, and Trikx kept his gaze on the tiefling. Seraphina was unmoved by this declaration and simply waited. “I just want this chapter of my life over. As far as I’m concerned, all three of them died ten years ago in that courtroom.”

The words lacked conviction. She felt it the moment they left her mouth. Ren looked at her, “We said we’d support you. But know, if you decide to let this go, there’s no turning back. His execution is scheduled for next month. You don’t leave soon, there won’t be time to do anything about it.”

“If I might interject,” Seraphina’s emotionless voice cut through the tension in the room and demanded the attention of the three of them. “You don’t need to make the decision to help or not right now. Go with them to Emberhold. Learn more about what’s happening. Don’t commit to anything. Decide when you must.”

That thought had not occurred to Elara. The thought of traveling with her parents made the blood retreat from her fingers. A cold tightness closed around her ribs, and she had to remind herself to breathe.

“You don’t have to go alone,” Ren said, putting her hand on Elara’s shoulder. “Assuming you want to go.”

“Yeah.” Trikx stood up, walked over to Elara, and looked up at her, “We’ll help you stack the deck this time.”

Elara returned Trikx's gaze. “[Noble] taught me that stories that aren’t written often don’t go how we expect. There are only two things you can rely on. Your own strength, and that control is an illusion. The only control we have over a story is how we tell it after it’s done. His death, and the Crown trying to blame me for it, has burned that lesson into me for eternity.”

Trikx narrowed his eyes and lowered his voice. He held his dagger out between himself and Ren. “You have strengths. I have strengths. Even she has strengths,” he shot a glance towards Ren before refocusing on Elara. “And yours is telling a story.” He stepped closer on his tiptoes, his voice dropping further so only Elara could hear, “That gift for shaping a tale is what kept me from an early grave.”

Ren moved to Elara’s other side. “Elara, let's go. The three of us. At the very least, you can close this chapter of your life in the manner of your choosing. If you don’t go, you’re going to wonder if the execution happened. You’ll forever be watching every door like Trikx. But instead of looking for danger, you’ll be expecting a ghost.”

Elara let out a quick breath, almost like a snort. “You two aren’t going to let this go, are you?” She hadn’t realized how tightly she’d been holding herself until this moment. As the tension left her shoulders and she breathed easier, the weight in her chest shifted. Not gone, just lighter.

“Be wary,” Seraphina warned. “Our familiars can hold more sway over us than we realize.”

Elara turned to Ren, “I’m not like them.” The words caught slightly in her throat.

Trikx began to speak, but Ren cut in before he could. “No, you aren’t. Because you choose to live your life on the level. You told the magistrate you would make your own way, and you did. You didn’t steal when you were starving. You didn’t bend the court with your magic when [Noble] died. You chose to rise above your past instead. You can choose to do that again, Elara.”

There was a problem with that. She already knew what she wanted to do, and she didn’t like what it said about her. Besides, she needed answers.
